Chemistry lab · 40-minute bench protocol

Grow crystals and split pen inks

Two experiments in one class. Dissolve copper sulfate in hot water and let it cool into crystals. While it cools, run pen ink up two kinds of paper in the same cup. Each experiment has its own tab, and the page tells you when to switch.

blue = copper sulfate, crystalsorange = chromatography Class of 14: four groups of 3 and one group of 2.

How the 40 minutes work

Crystals and chromatography both spend most of their time waiting. So you do not do one, then the other. You start one, and use its waiting time for the other. The clock at the top buzzes a message at each switch and highlights the tab to open.

Teacher prep (before class)
  • Put out CuSO₄·5H₂O in a labelled container for the class (about 200 g: 5 teams × 34 g, plus spare), with a spoon. It should include some clear crystals of 3–5 mm for seeds.
  • Set up one or two balances (0.1 g is fine) with weighing boats or paper. Students weigh 34 g themselves, so check the first team. With one balance, teams queue: the Chemist weighs while the others start Experiment 2.
  • If your CuSO₄ is only fine powder, make seeds the day before: leave a few drops of the saturated solution to evaporate on a slide, or leave a spoonful of solution in a saucer overnight, and pick out the best small crystals. Keep a few spare seeds.
  • Seeds go in only when the cup is warm, not hot (about 40–45 °C). A hot solution dissolves them.
  • Boil a kettle. Each group needs 50 mL of water at 60–70 °C, poured when the Chemist is ready at minute 5. Keep spare hot water for extra 5 mL top-ups.
  • 34 g in 50 mL keeps the same concentration as before (saturated at about 50 °C). If you can only spare 27 g per team, still use 50 mL of water but hang the seed only once the cup is below about 40 °C.
  • Solvents: about 150 mL of 90% ethanol for cup 1, and about 150 mL of a 1:1 mix of ethyl acetate and 90% ethanol for cup 2 (75 mL + 75 mL; 5 groups × 25 mL, plus spare). Make the mix in a ventilated room, shake it, and pour it as late as you can (minutes 9–12), because it evaporates fast. Keep both bottles capped.
  • Label one waste bottle “copper waste” and another “solvent waste” for leftover ethanol and ethyl acetate. Do not pour either down the drain.
  • Pens: students pick any four colours, so put out a mix of felt-tip pens, permanent or whiteboard markers and ballpoints. Water-based inks usually run well in ethanol, and may run less far in the less polar ethyl acetate mix, while permanent marker inks tend to run in both. A colour that does not move is a valid result (Rf = 0), but test a few pens first so you know what to expect.
  • Use glass cups, jars or beakers: ethyl acetate (even in the mix) softens polystyrene (clear disposable cups) and some other plastics. Each cup needs to be about 10 cm wide so two 4 cm strips hang side by side, and tall enough for a 10 cm strip to hang with only its bottom edge in a thin layer of liquid. If your cups are narrower, use 3 cm strips with the dots 0.7 cm apart. If they are shorter, trim the strips to 8 cm.
  • Cut strips: 10 A4 and 10 filter paper, 4 cm × 10 cm, plus spares.

Minutes 5–13, with checks at 17 and 20 · Experiment 1

Crystallization

Dissolve copper sulfate in hot water, let it cool a little, then hang a seed crystal in the middle of the solution. The salt cannot stay dissolved as the water cools, so it builds new crystal onto the seed.

Data card 34 g CuSO₄·5H₂O50 mL hot water, 60–70 °Cstir 2–3 min1 seed crystal on a thread

Goggles and gloves on. Pour hot water slowly. Copper solution goes in the waste bottle, not the sink.

    Crystals formed = solubility at 60 °C minus solubility at 20 °C. Your cup holds about 50 g of water, so multiply by 0.5.

    Why wait before hanging the seed? 34 g in 50 g of water is only saturated at about 50 °C. Above that the solution can still dissolve more, so a hot solution would dissolve your seed. Below it, the solution holds more than it can keep, so crystal builds onto the seed.

    1ESide question: why CuSO₄ and not NaCl?

    Table salt is cheaper and safer, yet we crystallize copper sulfate. Tick every statement you think is a good explanation, then check.

    Minutes 5–32 · Experiment 2

    Paper chromatography

    Put four pen colours of your choice on A4 paper and on filter paper, and run both papers in the same cup. Do it twice: once with 90% ethanol and once with a 1:1 mix of ethyl acetate and ethanol. Compare how far each solvent carries your colours and how well each paper separates them.

    Ethanol and ethyl acetate are flammable. No flames or heaters nearby. The ethyl acetate mix has strong fumes: the teacher pours it, keep that cup covered, and work near an open window or fan.

      2CRecord your measurements minute 26–32

      Measure in cm from the baseline. Measure each solvent front once. Then, for each colour, measure the spot that travelled furthest (top) and the one that travelled least (bottom). One spot only? Type the same distance twice. Colour never left the baseline? Type 0. Rf = spot distance ÷ front distance. Spread = Rf top minus Rf bottom: a bigger spread means the colour split into more distinct dyes.

      ColourTop spot (cm)Bottom spot (cm)Rf topRf bottomSpread

      A ⚠ means Rf is above 1, so check your measurement. Rf = 0 means that colour did not dissolve and travel in that solvent. That is a result, not a mistake.
